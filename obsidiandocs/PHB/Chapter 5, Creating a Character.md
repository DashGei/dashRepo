
**Your character is the vehicle through which you interact with the DM's world. Every character is a combination of class components, backstory, and your personal flair.**

## Who are you?

There are many places to start when creating a character, but the method this book recommends ensures that your character is fun for you, your party, and the DM. This section is tailored primary to my (Awesomecool Dashiell Geiser's) tastes, but most fantasy TTRPG tables generally function similar to mine.

Communicate and consider with your DM and fellow players what you want the general mood and genre to be for the game. My personal favorite is somewhere between the Resident Evil and Metal Gear series.

First, I recommend choosing a personality for your character which you believe would be fun for you and the rest of the table. Not all character ideas are built for the table, extremely ideological, anti-social, or chaotic characters can easily create schisms at the table which make it difficult to continue the game. Remember that creating a mechanically sound and competent character is quite easy, but making a character that fits your table is more of an investment.

The method which I (Awesomecool Dashiell Geiser) find best for testing whether or not your character will be enjoyable at the table is this:

### Dash's really awesome method of character personality and background and origin and such.

Imagine yourself at a party or other social gathering. You should be able to imagine yourself interacting with your character, and later deciding that although you had a nice talk, you can't see yourself interacting with them much intentionally. They might have been a little too socially awkward, self absorbed, loud, or maybe nothing totally clicked.

This ensures that your character will be able to function well with the other people at your table, but it also means your character won't just be yourself. Never make a character that would not join up with your other party members.

**Next** choose your character's background. Why are they looking out for adventure? Do they seek to indiscriminately aid the good people of the world? Do they require money to pursue a personal goal? Are they looking for a person or magical artifact?

Your character's personality is also important at this step, what led to them behaving they way they do? Why do they react to the world in their own unique way? Think about the answers to some of the following questions:

- Who raised you and how?
    
- What was your proudest moment?
    
- What was your most shameful?
    
- What organizations have you interacted with?
    
- Are you friend or foe to them?
    
- What keeps you going when things get tough?

**Now** choose some of the following items from the below tables.

### Experiences

- Handy. You gain proficiency in one Tool of your choice.
    
- Speaker. You learn one language of your choice.
    
- Scholar. You learn to read and write in one language you know.
    
- Devotee. Choose a deity, the party cannot have a negative standing with that deity
    
- One Contact. Work with your GM to establish one specific person or family in a city or region of your choice. This person owes you a minor favor and is generally willing to help you with information, shelter, or a small risk.
    
- Guild Membership. Work with your DM to choose a guild or trade organization. Members of that organization recognize you as one of their own and will generally provide basic lodging, meals, local information, or professional advice when reasonable.
    
- Temple Standing. Choose a religion or temple organization. Members of that faith generally provide you food, shelter, and basic assistance when able, provided you do not act against the interests of the faith.
    
- Military Service. You once served in an organized military force. You have Advantage on checks to identify military ranks, procedures, insignia, and tactics.
    
- Illicit Contact. Work with your GM to establish one fence or black-market trader. This contact is generally willing to buy unusual goods, smuggle items, or people.
    
- Alchemist. You add 1d6 to Intelligence or Medicine checks to identify a potion, oil, or alchemical substance by sight or smell.
    
- Natural Affinity. You add 1d6 to Wisdom (Animal Handling) checks with four beasts of your choice.
    
- Strong Swimmer. You have a swimming speed equal to your walking speed.
    
- Sea Legs. You add 1d6 to checks to avoid falling or being knocked prone on a moving ship or slippery surface.
    
- Weather Eye. After observing the sky and wind for 10 minutes, you can predict natural weather conditions for the next 12 hours with total accuracy, or you know if weather is unnatural.
    
- Streetwise. You add 1d6 to Charisma checks to gather rumors or find someone willing to buy or sell illegal goods.

    
- Bluff. You add 1d6 to Charisma (Deception) checks to bluff during a game or tense social interaction.
    
- Haggle. you add 1d6 to Charisma checks to negotiate prices when buying or selling goods.
    
- Fair Price. You automatically know the approximate fair market value of any common trade good (food, cloth, tools, etc.). For rare or exotic items, you have advantage on checks to estimate value.
    
- Scout's Eye. When you are the lead traveler (scouting ahead), you add 1d6 to Perception checks to spot ambushes or hazards.
    
- Experienced Performer. To take this experience you must already have the Hobby Performer experience. You gain proficiency with one musical instrument of your choice, and when you roll below an 8 on the D20 test of a performance check you treat the roll as an 8.
    
- Captivate. When you perform for at least 1 minute, you can cause up to six intelligent creatures who watch you to have disadvantage on Perception checks to notice anything other than your performance, until you stop or they are interrupted.
    
- Sacred Texts. You add 1d6 to Intelligence checks to recall information about your religion's history, figures, and myths.
    
- Smith. During a long rest, you can create a temporary forge assuming you have access to roughly enough materials.
    
- Worldwise. You add 1d6 to Charisma and Intelligence checks to understand and navigate rural cultural differences in etiquette or custom.
    
- Squire's Aid. Another creature can reduce the time it takes you to put on heavy armor by half if they use the help action.
    
- Chivalric Court. You don't have disadvantage on Charisma checks when interacting with hostile nobles, knights, and other members of the gentry. You are fluent in the etiquette of courts, feasts, and duels.
    
- Watchful. You add 1d6 to Perception checks while keeping watch during a long or short rest.
    
- Hobby Performer. You gain proficiency in one musical instrument, additionally you may use Dexterity or Wisdom instead of Charisma to make a performance check, but you roll 3d4 instead of a d20.
    
- Home Ground. Choose a region type (forest, mountains, swamp, desert, etc.). In that terrain, you cannot become lost except by magical means, and you add 1d6 to checks to find food, water, or shelter.
    
- Pathfinder. When you travel through wilderness, you and your group move at a normal pace without suffering penalties to stealth or perception, and your group's tracks are more difficult to follow (creatures tracking you have disadvantage on their checks).
    
- Befriend the Unnatural. You can use Animal Handling checks on any creature with -4 Intelligence or less.
    
- Alert. Even when you are engaged in another activity while traveling (such as foraging, navigating, or tracking), you remain alert to danger.
    
- Hunter. While tracking other creatures, you also learn their exact number, their sizes, and how long ago they passed through the area.
    
- Mimic. You can accurately mimic the sounds (not speech) of a creature you have heard. A creature who hears it makes an Insight check against your Mental DC. If the sounds are those produced by a beast, the check has Disadvantage
    


## Class and Attributes

This step is what determines the majority of how your character interacts mechanically with the world and in combat.


# Choose a Class

This system has a total of 14 classes to choose from, when choosing a class it is recommended to make your choice based simply on how much fun you think that class sounds to play. Class diversity isn't necessary for a party to function, but in general it is good to have at least one party member from two separate roles. 

Also consider the source of a class's power, a Martial class has no spell casting, a Mystic has some spellcasting meant to supplement their other features, and a Spellcaster's main mode of interaction with the world is through spellcraft.

# Fighting Styles

A @Fighting Style represents the techniques, training, and battlefield habits that define how your character approaches challenges.

All choose a Fighting Style at 1st level. Your choice grants a passive benefit immediately and helps shape your character's role throughout the campaign.

A Fighting Style also grants a permanent Health Bonus, which increases your maximum Hit Points at 1st level and whenever you gain a level.

At 7th level, your Fighting Style grants a special feat. This feat does not count against your Feats Known, does not require a Feat Slot to use, and can be used even if it does not appear on your class feat list or if your class does not use feats.

You must still obey the normal limit of activating no more than one feat on your turn.

### Fighting Style Requirements

Many Fighting Styles require specific equipment or circumstances to gain their benefits.

If a Fighting Style lists a requirement, you only gain its benefits while meeting that requirement.

For example, a character with the @Dueling Fighting Style gains its benefits only while one hand remains free, while a character with the @Polearm Fighting Style gains its benefits only while wielding a weapon with the Reach property.

### Health Bonuses

Each Fighting Style grants either a +2 or +3 Health Bonus.

When you gain a level, increase your maximum Hit Points by the listed amount.

## Ability Scores

@Ability Scores represent your character's capabilities and failings.

To determine your character's ability scores, you purchase 5 of the scores in the graph and assign each to one of your ability modifiers. You have a total of 13 purchasing points, and the lowest a score can go is -1.

| Score | -1  | 0   | 1   | 2   | 3   |
| ----- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cost  | 0   | 1   | 2   | 3   | 5   |

| -3 points | -2 points | -3 point     | -5 points | 0 points |

Pay attention to the Physical and Mental modifiers used by the class you chose, those are the scores you want to be the highest. One is usually 3 and the other is usually 2.

A Physical Modifier is Strength or Dexterity. 

A Mental Modifier is Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma.

## Race

After working out your background and experiences, you choose a race for your character. Each race gives your character its own special bonuses and determines what they may look like.

When choosing a race, it is recommended to pick one which highlights important parts of your character's traits. For example your big hearted and larger than life Paladin of Sol may be significantly enhanced if you choose to make them an Orc. Your practical naturalist may have a much more graspable character if they are a Moor Elf or Human, leading to meaningful interactions with the world.

### Human

Spanning every continent, humans are the most diverse and ancient of all mortals. Humankind first evolved from apes, eventually spreading throughout the world and creating the first glimmers of society.

Being one of the two populations not born of gods, humans typically value self determination, resourcefulness, and the natural world. In fact while humans make up about 65% of all surface dwellers, they're only roughly 30% of city dwellers, preferring to stick to hunter gatherer communities and small townships.

Human Traits

- **Size**: Medium (about 5'3" – 5'10")

- **Age**: Humans typically live 50-70 years

- **Speed**: 30 feet walking

- **Resourceful**: Increase any ability score by 1, gain proficiency in one skill which uses said ability score.

- **Primordial**: You can communicate simple ideas to any Beast, or Elemental. It can understand your words, though you have no special ability to understand it in return.

Optional Character Traits and Trivia

- Many humans do not believe in fate.

- Many humans wear face and body paints to connect themselves with the Primordial titans.

- Some humans may despise their natural origins and seek religious rebirth.

### Halfling

Primarily residing in the west of their home continent Dvalum, Halflings tend to their gardens and hearths. Stories are spun of their unfathomable ability to remain content and happy with the simple pleasures of life.

Halflings are rarely known to take up adventuring, so they may expect the odd glance from other travelers. Despite these stigmas Halflings can be extraordinarily capable, their small frame giving them the ability to disappear whenever they please, and their patron god Tymora is known to give them a helping hand whenever things get tricky.

Halfling Traits

- **Size**: Small (about 3'3" – 4'6")

- **Age**: Halflings typically live 60-80 years

- **Speed**: 30 feet walking

- **Naturally Stealthy**: You can take the hide action when within 5 feet of another creature who is larger than you.

- **Luck**: When you roll a 1 on the d20 of a D20 Test, you can reroll the die, on a roll of 12-20 you treat the roll as if it was a 20. If you roll 1-11 you automatically fail the d20 test.

Optional Character Traits and Trivia

- The greatest sin a Halfling can commit is that of being impolite.

### Dwarf

Many scholars debate the location of the long lost Dwarvish homeland, whether it resides in the far northern tundras of Pol, in the heart of the great Joursk mountains, or somewhere deep in the howling underdarks of the world. One thing that is known for certain is that a Dwarf's love for good stories, liquor, and a well balanced axe is second to none.

Squat and rosy cheeked, Dwarves are incredibly resilient, and their cultures value quality of craft and character equally. Molded by Hephaestus from the stones of the world, a Dwarf has an uncanny ability to find their way in caves.

Dwarf Traits

- **Size**: Small (about 4'0" – 5'3")

- **Age**: Dwarves typically live 60-80 years

- **Speed**: 30 feet walking

- **Dwarven Resilience**: You have Resistance to Poison damage. You also have Advantage on saving throws you make to avoid or end the Poisoned condition.

- **Stonecunning**: When standing on a stone surface, you have[[Exploration#Vision | Darkvision]] out to a range of 120 feet, and[[Exploration#Vision | Tremorsense]] out to a range of 10 feet.

Optional Character Traits and Trivia

- Mountain Dwarves, both male and female alike, are known for their extravagant beards.

- The typical greeting between Dwarves of two different houses includes an exchange and inspection of weaponry.

### Orc

Few mortals can hold a candle to the raw power of an Orc who truly believes in what they're fighting for. Orcs can often be found in important societal positions, where they put their skills as natural and captivating leaders to use.

Hale, grey skinned with piercing yellow eyes, Orcs were created by the first sun god Sol, who blessed his children with honorable fervor. Orcs are often deeply passionate and defensive, and while their origins stem from a virtuous god, Orcs can be deeply aligned with any ideals.

Orc Traits

- **Size**: Medium (about 5'6" - 6'3")

- **Age**: Orcs typically live 50-70 years

- **Speed**: 30 feet walking

- **Radiant Resilience**: You have resistance to Radiant damage.

- **Purge the Unholy**: When you deal damage to an undead creature, you can change the damage type of the attack to radiant.

- **Sun Child**: When you are in the light of Sol on the start of your turn, your speed increases by 10 feet. Taking a short rest when in the light of Sol grants you an amount of temporary hit points equal to PB x 2. When these hit points are lost, up to two creatures of your choice within 5 feet of you take Radiant damage equal to your level.

Optional Character Traits and Trivia

- Tattoos are very important to Orcish society, most of which display scenes of dawn or Sol's animal avatar the boar.

- Some of the oldest legends tell tales of the two sun Deities Sol and Ea'or, and many of these stories have been adapted to feature heroic tales of Orcish knights saving delicate Elves.

### Elf

Elves bear pointed ears along with a penchant for fine wine and arts. Many Elves can be described as shrewd or astute folk, who's dedication to mastery of a given skill is palpable. Despite their somewhat cold veneer, Elves are known to be quite poetic and expressive in the right company.

Their creator and second sun deity Ea'or holds domain over the ideas of passion and aesthetics, and these traits are pillars of Elven society. While this indulgence has created a tight-knit culture of philosophers and savants, a notable amount of Elves harbor xenophobic tendencies when interacting with cultures they view as less artistic.

Elf Traits

- **Size**: Medium (about 6'0 - 6'6")

- **Age**: Elves can live up to 200 years, but most die around the ages of 90 - 120

- **Speed**: 30 feet walking

- **Silver Sight**: When in moonlight, you have[[Exploration#Vision | Darkvision]] out to 1 mile, and advantage on attack rolls. Additionally when you take a long rest in direct moonlight, you progress 4 hours of the rest for every hour that passes.

- **Dedicated Practice**: You gain proficiency with a chosen tool or skill, if you already have proficiency with said choice you gain expertise.

- **Subrace**: The Elves of the world come from different backgrounds, and overtime they have adapted to their surroundings, pick one subrace which aligns with your Elf's origins, you gain the features of that subrace.

Optional Character Traits and Trivia

- Some of the oldest legends tell tales of the two sun Deities Sol and Ea'or, and many of these stories have been adapted to feature heroic tales of Orcish knights saving delicate Elves.

- Elvish is a unique language in that it does not feature hard consonants such as B, G, T, K, D, or P. Elves sometimes have trouble pronouncing words with these sounds.

- Male and Female Elves are typically the same height. This extends further with High Elves who to most, sexual dimorphism is entirely imperceptible.

- At night Ea'or's moon daughter Lumiere reflects their light so that Elves may see in the dark.

- In traditional Elvish culture, at the age of 20 an Elf is to practice a chosen discipline for one decade, after which they are considered an adult.

- Dreams are the most important thing in the world to Deep Elves, and they are habitual journalists in an effort to unlock what may be the secret to the foundations of the multiverse.

Subraces

- **High Elf**: High Elves have dark straight hair and bright orange eyes, hailing from the dunes of the Sym of which their nation shares the same name. The Sym's new leader Serale has declared herself god empress and seeks to control the whole of the the continent by ousting or decimation of all other races. Your High Elf may or may not share the same ideals.

- **High Elf Magic**: At level 1 you know the Prestidigitation Cantrip, and at level 5 you learn the Moonbeam spell, it is always prepared and you can cast it once per long rest without expending a spell slot.

- **Moor Elf**: Moor Elves are characterized by their striking purple eyes, blonde hair, and connection to the grounded aspects of the world. To many Moor Elves, physical practices such as archery, pathfinding, textiles, and horseback are their own artistic disciplines. Most Moor Elves live on the outskirts of suburbs in the northern half of the continent Y'Valm.

- **Moor Elf Pioneering**: At level 1 your base speed increases by 10 feet. At level 5 you learn one single Tier 1 or 2 Feat with the Exploration tag. Once per Short Rest you can use that Feat without expending a Feat Slot.

- **Sea Elf**: Sea elves live in the oceans of the world along with other aquatic races, generally more curious and free spirited in comparison with their land dwelling cousins, Sea Elves have blue skin with black hair and freckles with lively green eyes.

### Gnome

Gnomes are a curious group of people, mostly known by the other folk of the world by the various seals stamped into equipment produced and designed by them. The most praised member of any Gnomish family is whoever develops the most efficient and successful invention, and these patents are guarded as a Red Dragon might with their gold.

Folk who have never met a Gnome may be surprised to be greeted by a short statured individual with an unparalleled zest for life. Many famous and infamous companies and businesses are headed by Gnomes, spanning large swaths of continents.

Gnome Traits

- **Size**: Small (about 3'3" – 4'6")

- **Age**: Gnomes typically live 80-90 years

- **Speed**: 30 feet walking

- **Eyes for Quality**: You have Advantage on Ability Checks and Saving Throws against spells of the Illusion school. You also have proficiency in any item created by a Gnome.

- **Studied**: You know an additional language and gain proficiency in one Intelligence skill along with two tools or instruments of your choice.

Drow

Of all the civilizations on Gaia, none is more feared than that of the Drow. Those who die defending villages from their raids are often considered the lucky ones, as Drow are known slavers and sadists. Lolth gave her followers dark purple skin, silver hair, and bright red eyes to strike fear in the hearts of their victims.

Long ago when an Elvish enclave came to the middle forests of Dvalum, they forsook their patron deity Ea'or in favor of the malefic spider queen Lolth. Now residing in the deep caverns of the Underdark, Drow stalk the narrow corridors and wait until the moonlight has dimmed to ambush surface dwellers.

# Build Your Character!

You now have everything to start building your character, get a character sheet and follow the next steps.

 1. Fill in each of the 5 Ability scores at the top of your sheet
      
 2. Look at this section of your class's sheet which should look something like this![[Pasted image 20260621165511.png]]
 3. Mark your character's Physical and Mental Modifiers with an X below them.
     
 4. Choose your class's @Fighting Style, this effects the amount of HP you will have.
     
 4. Calculate your level 1 hit points.
     
 5. Write your armor and weapon proficiencies.
     
 6. Mark your saving throw proficiencies by adding the modifier and your proficiency bonus if proficient.
     
 7. Mark your class's skill proficiencies alongside the skills you gained from your background
     
 8. Fill in the 5 Ability Scores you purchased
     
 9. Calculate your skill bonuses by adding the associated ability modifier along with your proficiency bonus if you have proficiency.
     
 10. Write down your Physical and Mental DCs by doing:
 
  Physical DC = 8 + Proficiency Bonus + Physical Modifier

  Mental DC = 8 + Proficiency Bonus + Mental Modifier
  
 11. Write your attack bonuses by doing:

  Melee Attack Bonus = Strength + Proficiency Bonus

  Ranged Attack Bonus = Dexterity + Proficiency Bonus

  Spell Attack Bonus = Mental Modifier + Proficiency Bonus
  
 12. Write down your spellcasting features. If your class gives you spell casting abilities at level 1, the dropdown table on your class sheet shows you what spells you have at what levels and your class tells you what spell list you can draw from.
