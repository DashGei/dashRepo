To keep the game running as smooth as possible, it is important to delegate which parts of the game are kept track of by which players, and who is responsible for knowing certain rules.

**Sometimes** a session might have or call for an extended period of time between social or combat encounters during this period characters have time to explore the world, for connections, and work on their equipment.

This chapter also covers the powers and mechanics the entire party gain access to at certain levels once they become fully fledged adventurers.

# Leveling up

When the party accomplishes a great task, or possibly before a major plot point, the DM lets the party level up (usually the next time they take a long rest).


# Level 2, Crafting Equipment

Using the rules below, characters can make nonmagical items, Potions of Healing, and Spell Scrolls.

## Crafting Nonmagical Items

To craft a nonmagical item, you need tools, raw materials, and time, each of which is detailed below. If you meet the requirements, you make the item, and you can use it or sell it at its normal price.

### Tools

The equipment chapter's **Tools** section lists which tools are required to make certain items. The DM assigns required tools for items not listed there. Some tools, especially those which require metal, might require a workstation such as a forge determined by the DM.

You must use the required tool to make an item and have proficiency with that tool. Anyone who helps you must also have proficiency with it.

### Raw Materials

To make an item, you need raw materials worth half its purchase cost (round down). For example, you need 750 GP of raw materials to make Plate Armor, which sells for 1,500 GP. The DM determines whether appropriate raw materials are available.

### Time

To determine how many days (working 8 hours a day) it takes to make an item, divide its purchase cost in GP by 10 (round a fraction up to a day). For example, you need 5 days to make a Crossbow, which sells for 50 GP.

If an item requires multiple days, the days needn’t be consecutive.

Characters can combine their efforts to shorten the crafting time. Divide the time needed to create an item by the number of characters working on it. Normally, only one other character can assist you, but the DM might allow more assistants.

## Brewing Potions of Healing

A character who has proficiency with the **Herbalism Kit** can create a **Potion of Healing**.

Doing so requires:

- A Herbalism Kit
- **25 GP** of raw materials
- **1 day** (8 hours of work)

Upon completion, you create one **Potion of Healing**.

# Scribing Spell Scrolls

A spellcaster can transfer a spell to a scroll and create a **Spell Scroll**, using the rules below.

## Time and Cost

Scribing a scroll takes an amount of time and money based on the level of the spell, as shown in the **Spell Scroll Costs** table.

For each day of inscription, you must work for **8 hours**. If a scroll requires multiple days, those days needn't be consecutive.

## Prerequisites for the Scribe

To scribe a scroll, you must have proficiency in the Arcana skill or with Calligrapher's Supplies and have the spell prepared on each day of the inscription. You must also have at hand any Material components required by the spell; if the spell consumes its Material components, they are consumed only when you complete the scroll. The scroll’s spell uses your spell save DC and spell attack bonus.

## Cantrips

If the scribed spell is a cantrip, the version on the scroll works as if the caster were your level.

# Level 4, Mentorship

When the party reaches level 4, they are able to share with each other their skills and knowledge. During a long rest a party member (The Mentor) can impart a portion of their learned skills to another party member (The Student).

### Mentorship Process

1. **Initiation.** At the start of a long rest, the Mentor and Student declare they are spending the rest in focused training.
    
2. **Teaching.** The Mentor selects one proficiency or minor ability they possess, from the list below, to impart. The Student must lack that proficiency or ability.
    
3. **Rest Completion.** At the end of the long rest, the Student makes an ability check using the Mentoring ability, the DC is lowered by the Mentor's Intelligence Modifier.
    
    - **Success:** The Student gains the taught feature immediately. See below the table for what each lesson gives you.
        
    - **Failure:** The Student retains the partial training. They gain advantage on the next attempt to learn this same feature from the same Mentor. If they fail three times, the lesson is beyond them for now, and they cannot attempt to learn that specific feature from that Mentor again until they gain a level.

### What Can Be Taught?

| Category               | Examples                                      | Mentoring Ability                               | Mentoring DC |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| **Language**           | One language the Mentor speaks and writes in. | Intelligence                                    | 21           |
| **Tool Proficiency**   | One tool kit, vehicle, or gaming set          | Dexterity or Intelligence (appropriate to tool) | 18           |
| **Skill Proficiency**  | One skill the Mentor is proficient in         | The skill’s associated ability                  | 20           |
| **Cantrip**            | One cantrip the Mentor knows                  | The Mentor’s Mental Modifier                    | 21           |
| **Weapon Proficiency** | One weapon the Mentor is proficient in        | Dexterity or Strength                           | 18           |
| **Martial Feat**       | One Martial Feat from the Mentor's class list | The Mentor’s Intelligence or Wisdom Modifier    | 20           |
**Language**: The student adds the language to their languages known, or learns to write in that language if they already knew how to speak it.

**Tool**: The student has proficiency in the tool.

**Skill**: The student has half proficiency in that skill, adding half of their proficiency bonus to checks made with that skill.

**Cantrip**: That student learns the cantrip, using their Mental Modifier for attack rolls and spell save DC.

**Weapon Proficiency**: The student becomes proficient in the weapon.

**Martial Feat**: The student adds the learned Feat to their class's Feats known.

**Restrictions.**

- A Mentor can teach only one Student per long rest.
    
- A Student can learn from only one Mentor per long rest.
    
- A Student can learn at most **two** features through Mentorship. Once they have learned two, they can still serve as a Mentor, but cannot be a Student again.
    
- You cannot teach a feature that is granted by a class, feature, or background that you only have access to temporarily (e.g., from a spell effect or ranger aspect).


# Level 6, Divine Favor

**For many in the world of Gaia**, Religion plays a communal and utilitarian role in one's life. Rarely will individuals devote themselves to singular gods, or swear all of them off outright.

A typical peasant may pray to Gri in the morning for a safe crop, evoke Ilxi's name while gambling with their friends, and then go home where an emblem of Helm sits above the door and guards against evils.

### Player Interactions

Just like everyone else, players can form relationships with the various gods of the world, gaining boons, or possibly minor woes if they repeatedly offend some deities.

Not every god preferers the same method of worship. Some gods wish only for larger and larger masses of dedicated worshippers some simply want their follower's to practice their disciplines, and others care solely that their ideals are spread. 

### Divine Favor

The party loses or gains **Favor Points**  by supporting a deity's efforts and ideals as detailed in each deity's Favor Criteria section, at the DM's discretion unmentioned actions that the party takes may effect relationships with the gods.

Favor grants the entire party small, thematic blessings. Acting against a god’s principles can bring minor woes. A party who makes meaningful connections with each other and the world will always bring favor.

When the party finishes a long rest together, they may ask the DM to reveal their current standings with the gods. They may also choose to voluntarily decrease their Favor with any deity by any amount at that time to make room for other relationships.

### Favor Scale

A party’s standing with a deity ranges from -5 to +10.

| Favor Score | Status     | Effect      |
| ----------- | ---------- | ----------- |
| -5          | Disfavored | Greater Woe |
| -4 to -3    | Displeased | Minor Woe   |
| 0           | Neutral    | -           |
| 1 - 2       | Noticed    | -           |
| 3 - 5       | Favored    | Minor Boon  |
| 6 - 9       | Honored    | Boon        |
| 10          | Champion   | Major Boon  |

Favor Points adjust at the end of a long rest or session at the DM’s discretion. The party cannot lose or gain more than 5 FP per session with a given god through direct offense, and minor slights may not change the score at all.


The party can hold a maximum of 30 Favor Points invested across a maximum of 5 deities at any time. Negative Favor Points do not add to this total, and the party can have a maximum of 10 total negative Favor Points invested. If the party would gain FP that would push their total above 30, those points are lost.

### Worship Methods

Every deity has a **Worship Method** that dictates how the party gains Favor. The DM awards 1 FP when the party meaningfully fulfills the method, and occasionally 2 FP for exceptional acts.

| Method         | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Devotion**   | The god grows stronger through worship. FP is earned by spreading the faith, building shrines, converting otherss, or leading communal prayers.                                                                         |
| **Discipline** | The god values the practice of a craft, skill, or way of life. FP is earned by engaging in the associated discipline. Creating art, mastering a trade, or upholding a code may be the best way to earn this gods favor. |
| **Ideals**     | The god cares that their ideals are enacted. FP is earned by championing those ideals, defeating opposing forces, or shifting a community’s values.                                                                     |

### Boons & Woes

Boons are occasional minor bonuses that the party may obtain by maintaining good relationships with gods. Each god has their own set of boons, some may give players resources to spend during the day, some may only activate when the DM chooses.

Some Boons and Woes require the tracking of the Velthraen Calendar. Also if a Boon or Woe effects one party member a day or week, it only happens the time that party member triggers it, ignoring subsequent triggers by other party members until the Boon or Woe refreshes.

## Ea’or
_The Lovers, Patron of Passion and Aesthetics_

Ea'or  is the embodiment of the smaller, fuchsia star in the center of the planescape. Ea'or was created alongside their companion god Sol when the central star was split upon arrival of the gods. Ea'or is responsible for the creation of Elves, and their daughter goddess Lumiere who embodies the moon.

**Domains:** Beauty,  passion, twilight, Elves
**Worship Method:** Discipline (creating or appreciating art)

**Favor Criteria:**

- The party commissions, restores, or protects a significant work of art.
    
- Two or more party members share a bottle of wine in Ea’or’s name.
    
- The party thwarts a Drow or agent(s) of Lolth.

**Disfavor Criteria:**

- The party deliberately destroys an artwork.
    
- They side with forces that suppress artistic freedom.
    
- The party sides with Drow or agent(s) of Lolth.

### Boons

**Minor Boon (FP 3-5): Silver Tongues.** Party members re-roll any 1s on Charisma (Performance) checks.

**Boon (FP 6-9): Moonchild.** Once per long rest, a party member can cast the spell *Invisibility* without expending a spell slot.

**Major Boon (FP 10): Ea’or’s Muse.** Once per week (resetting on Same'ih), the party can complete a long rest in 4 hours instead of 8 if bathed in direct moonlight. Additionally, any Charisma ability checks made on the subsequent day gain a 1d4 bonus.

### Woes

**Minor Woe (FP -3 to -4): Jaded Eye.** The first Charisma check a party member makes per day suffers a –1d4 penalty.

**Greater Woe (FP -5): See How You Like It.** Once per week (resetting on Same'ih), none of the party's accomplishments are recognized in any meaningful way.

## Lumiere
_The Moon, Patron of Discipline_

Lumiere reflects her parent deity's light so that her followers may see through the night. To her and her devotees, every arrow loosed, silent footstep felled, and craft perfected is a work of art unto itself. 

**Domains:** Discipline, archery, precision, moonlight, perfection, mastery of the self, Moor Elves
**Worship Method:** **Discipline** (patient practice, precision, and quiet improvement.)


### Favor Criteria

- A party member spends downtime meditating, maintaining their bow, and fletching arrows.
    
- The party completes a difficult task through patience rather than speed.
    
- Two or more party members train together during a long rest or downtime.
    
- A party member willingly repeats a failed attempt until they succeed instead of abandoning it.

### Disfavor Criteria

- A party member abandons a worthy pursuit simply because it proves difficult.
    
- A party member knowingly neglects their equipment or training.

### Boons

**Minor Boon (FP 3-5): Moonlit Meditations.** Repeated failed mentorship checks no longer prevent party members from learning skills until level up.

**Boon (FP 6-9): Deft.** During nighttime, party members re roll 1s on ranged attack rolls with ranged weapon attacks.

**Major Boon (FP 10): Silver Sights.** The party has the Silver Sight racial bonus. An Elf receives the benefits even if they are not in moonlight.

### Woes

**Minor Woe (FP -3 to -4): Shaken Hands.** Once per day, the first time a party member makes an attack roll with a ranged weapon or a Dexterity-based tool check, subtract 1d4 from the roll.

**Greater Woe (FP -5): Under a Glass Moon.** During nighttime outdoors, the party has Disadvantage on Perception checks that rely on sight even in lit spaces.

## Frey
_The World, Patron of the Hearth_

Frey is the patron god of Halflings, homely ideals, and taking in strangers from the night. It isn't difficult to find signs of Frey worship and his (or sometimes her) followers are known to leave kind hearted messages above fireplaces or on the beds of inns they especially enjoyed. Many tavern-esque establishments leave offering tables to Frey in the middle of their main rooms.

**Domains:** Community, fertility, hospitality, Halflings
**Worship Method:** Devotion (building and maintaining communities, leading harvest rites)

**Favor Criteria:**

- They defend a farmstead or common folk from a threat.
    
- The party partakes in a communal meal or celebration in Frey’s name.
    
- The party invites one or more NPCs over for dinner.
    
- They convince others to honor Frey’s simple rituals.

**Disfavor Criteria:**

- They abandon a community in need when aid was within their power.
    
- They mock the value of hard, honest labor.
    
- The party is exceptionally impolite to people hosting them.

### Boons

**Minor Boon (FP 3-5): Hearty Meal.** Once per long rest, when the party finishes a short rest and consumes food, each member regains an extra 1d4 hit points.

**Boon (FP 6-9): Kind Beginnings.** Once per long rest, when the party meets a new NPC, 2d6 is added to the first Charisma check made with that creature.

**Major Boon (FP 10): Cornucopia.** Once per week (Resetting on Frejdag), when a party member opens an unoccupied room (such as a rented chamber, an abandoned cottage, or a cleared cellar), the DM may have a _Heroes’ Feast_ spell appear inside, laid out moments before their arrival. A card depicting a cornucopia with fine lettering reading, _“To my good friends, come and make merry,”_ rests plainly upon the feast.

### Woes

**Minor Woe (FP -3 to -4) No Quarter:** Once per week, when the party visits a hostel or inn, that inn is closed or otherwise unaccepting of guests.

**Greater Woe (FP -5) Bleugh:** Once per week at the DM's discretion, parties must make a DC 15 Strength saving throw or be poisoned by bad food for an hour upon waking.


## Sol
_The Sun, Patron of Valor_

Sol is the greater, golden star that warms the world and the patron god of Orcs. He appears as a towering male Orc in gleaming, gilded plate, or as a colossal boar whose tusks sweep low and wide. His followers champion righteousness through action, not words, and believe that the truest good is a life laid down for others.

**Domains:** Righteousness, valor, self‑sacrifice, dawn, Orcs  
**Worship Method:** Ideals (championing good through bravery and self‑sacrifice)

### Favor Criteria

- A party member takes a significant risk to protect an innocent or ally.
    
- The party defeats a powerful evil without resorting to dishonorable tactics.
    
- Two or more party members swear an oath together before a battle or important undertaking.
    
- The party defeats or thwarts an undead or fiendish creature.

### Disfavor Criteria

- The party flee a winnable fight.
    
- They break a sworn oath or promise made in good faith.
    
- A party member sacrifices another for personal gain or cowardice.

### Boons

**Minor Boon (FP 3-5):  Vigor.** Once per long rest, when a party member drops below half hitpoints, the spell Heroism is cast on them using that character's Mental Modifier

**Boon (FP 6-9): Glory Be.** Once per long rest, when a party member fails a saving throw against the frightened or charmed condition, the DM may allow them to succeed instead. If the condition was caused by an undead or fiend, the source takes radiant damage equal to your level.

**Major Boon (FP 10): Under The Sun.** Once per long rest, one party member can cast *Daylight*, or if they have direct sunlight, *Flame Strike* without the expenditure of a spell slot. 

### Woes

**Minor Woe (FP -3 to -4): Waning Courage.** Once per day, the first time one party member would gain the frightened condition, all party members within 15 feet of them must also make the same saving throw or suffer the full effects.

**Greater Woe (FP -5): Sensitive to Light.** While in direct sunlight, the party no longer benefits from healing during short rests. Additionally, undead, celestials, and fiendish creatures have advantage on attacks against them until dusk.

## Lucifer
_The Devil, Patron of Comeuppance_

Any and all evil creatures have made a pact with Lucifer whether they know it or not. The pact dictates eternal servitude and devotion,  in lieu of eternal damnation. The majority of Luciferic worshipers are tyrants and debt collectors, but some cults devoted to Lucifer believe they may be reborn  as cambions if they dedicate their lives to him.

**Domains:** Half‑truths, betrayal, debts, comeuppance, Hell, Infernal Tieflings  
**Worship Method:** Ideals (making deals and punishing those who break them)

### Favor Criteria

- The party lies for meaningful gain.
    
- The party successfully collects a debt from someone.
    
- The party wins against a foe by underhanded tactics.
    
- A party member traps an enemy in the words of their own promise.
    
- The party turns an enemy’s betrayal back upon them.

### Disfavor Criteria

- A party member tells the truth to an outside influence when that truth directly harms another party member.
    
- The party forgives a significant debt without extracting any payment or punishment.
    
- A party member breaks the letter of a freely made agreement.
    
- the party aids Demons.

### Boons

**Minor Boon (FP 3-5): Sympathy for the Devil.** Party members re-roll any 1s on Deception checks.

**Boon (FP 6-9): Sinister Purpose.** Once per long rest, when a party member is reduced to 0 hit points. The creature that damaged them becomes marked with an invisible sigil; the party knows its direction for 1 hour. If the marked creature is reduced to 0 hit points before the hour ends, the party member regains PB x 1d6 hitpoints.

**Major Boon (FP 10): Hellish Arbiter.** Once per week (resetting on Lucae), the party may make a pact: When an NPC willingly signs a written agreement without magical influence a Cambion appears and writes his own name alongside the NPC's. If the NPC ever breaks this agreement they are immediately reduced to 1 hitpoint and teleported to the party's location with the restrained condition for an hour as they are bound by hot iron chains. A creature bound in this way in unable to verbalize anything but truths. This part of the pact must be stated in the agreement.

### Woes

**Minor Woe (FP -3 to -4): Turn of the Screw.** Party members never have Advantage on Charisma checks against Devils.

**Greater Woe (FP -5): Mark of the Default.** A faint, infernal brand appears on the party’s palms, visible only to fiends and those who deal in souls. Devils and other lawful evil outsiders know if the party tells a lie to them, and their attacks add an additional 1d4 fire damage to party members.

### Disfavor Criteria

- The party aids Devils.
    
- The party lets arbitrary rules prevent them from achieving their goals.

**Boon (FP 6-9): Fortified Veins.** When a party member has the Poisoned condition they do not have disadvantage on ability checks.

**Major Boon (FP 10): Blanked.** Once per day, when a party member lands a critical hit on an enemy that creature is effected by the *Crown of Madness* spell until the start of their next turn.

### Woes

**Minor Woe (FP -3 to -4): Mind Blight.** While poisoned, a party member also has disadvantage on Wisdom saving throws.

_The High Priestess, Patron of Fear_

Devils and Demons are a dime a dozen, but the rarer, more powerful children of Ur come in all shapes and sizes depending on the phobia they represent. Many are powerful enough to be considered demi-gods such as Lolth or Hastur, though they all originate from the plane of fear known as the Shadowfell.

**Domains:** Fear, the unknown, Maels
**Worship Method:** Devotion (Spreading  fear.)

### Disfavor Criteria

- The party succumbs to their fears.
    
- The party panics at inopportune moments.
    
- They permanently banish a Mael.

### Boons

- **Boon (FP 6-9): Masters of Fear.** Enemies making saving throws against the Frightened condition subtract 1d4 from their saving throws.
    
- **Major Boon (FP 10): Mind Warp.** Once per week (resetting on Lucae), a party member can cast the spell *Fear*.

### Woes

**Minor Woe (FP -3 to -4): In the Shadows.** When  the party takes a short or long rest the DM rolls 1d12 in secret. The party’s first attempt to take a short rest during this storm is interrupted by a sudden, deafening thunderclap.

## Aeon

_Primordial Titan of Earth, the Unmovable Object

Aeon is the oldest being in the planescape, the great stone Gaia is just an extension of themself, now cast off as they reside in their extraplanar prison. The other Titans view the great Wyrm as simple, but Aeon processes time at a much quicker rate than all others leading them to view all things as fickle.

**Domains:** Earth, stone, mountains, stability, endurance 
**Worship Method:** Ideals (defending nature and the earth’s integrity)

### Favor Criteria

- The party prevents a mining operation that threatens a sacred mountain, cavern, or underground ecosystem.
    
- They stop a creature or force that unnaturally destabilizes the ground (subsidence, magical earthquakes, burrowing horrors that collapse settlements).
    
- Two or more party members meditate in a cave or on bare rock for a full watch.


### Disfavor Criteria

- The party deliberately collapses a cave or stone structure that holds natural beauty or homes.
    
- They desecrate an ancient menhir circle, burial cairn, or petrified forest.
    
- The party abuses wildlife residing in caves or soil.


### Boons

- **Minor Boon (FP 3-5): Surefooted.** Party members have Advantage on saving throws against the prone condition as long as they are standing on natural stone.
    
- **Boon (FP 6-9): Earthen Eyes.** If a party member closes their eyes and meditates calmly for one hour, they gain Tremorsense out to 500 feet while doing nothing but meditating.
    
- **Major Boon (FP 10): Turn to Stone.** Once per week (resetting on Bahaphay), the party can cast the spell _Meld into Stone_ as a ritual, affecting the entire party at once. While merged, they can sense the vibrations of any creature moving on the stone within 100 feet. Additionally, once during that merge, one party member can cast _Stoneskin_ on themselves without expending a spell slot.


### Woes

- **Minor Woe (FP -3 to -4): Loose Ground.** Once per day, when a party member takes the Dash action on natural ground, the DM may have the earth shift underfoot. The character must succeed on a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw or fall prone.
    
- **Greater Woe (FP -5): Trembling Earth.** Once per week (resetting on Prydei), when the party takes a long rest in a natural cave, ruin, or overland camp, the earth shakes violently for 1 minute. The tremor extinguishes open flames, scatters loose gear, and forces a DC 15 Constitution saving throw from each party member to gain any benefit from the rest. On a failure, the rest counts as a short rest instead.
