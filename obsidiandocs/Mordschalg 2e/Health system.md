Also allows us to remove AC and therefore attack rolls, "misses" are represented by chunking into stamina and forcing the enemy to dodge/parry, hits happen once a creature runs out of the stamina required to defend themself and you hit their body.

## Core Principle

There is no attack roll and no Armor Class.

When you attack, you roll damage. The damage result determines what happens to the target’s health pools.

Most misses still cost the defender stamina, because they had to parry, dodge, twist, or retreat. A solid hit is not a small subtraction from a giant HP pool; it is a wound.


## 1. Damage Resolution

### Step 1: Roll Damage

Roll the weapon’s damage dice and add its flat damage bonus.

Weapon properties, talents, and special abilities can increase or reroll this damage.

### Step 2: Compare to Physical Defense

Every creature has a **Physical Defense (PD)**.

Baseline human PD is **6**.

Compare the damage total to the target’s PD.

| Result     | Damage Category | Points Applied        |
| ---------- | --------------- | --------------------- |
| Total < PD | Miss            | 1 HP, only to Stamina |
| PDx1       | Minor           | 1 HP                  |
| PDx1.5     | Major           | 2 HP                  |
| PDx2       | Severe          | 3 HP                  |

### Step 3: Apply to Health Pools

Physical damage is applied in this order:

> **Stamina → Armor Gate → Flesh → Vitality**



## 2. Physical Health Pools

### Stamina

Stamina represents dodging, parrying, rolling, twisting away, and otherwise preventing a weapon from meeting your body.

- A normal human has **3 Stamina**.
    
- Skirmishers and duelists may have 4–6.
    
- Heavily armored fighters often have less Stamina because they rely on armor.

**Effects of Stamina loss:**

- Stamina loss does not cause wounds.
    
- When Stamina reaches 0, you are too tired to properly defend yourself. You cannot take reactions that depend on movement or defense.
    
- You may still attack, but you are vulnerable.

**Stamina recovery:**

- At the start of your turn, if you have not lost any Stamina since the end of your previous turn, regain **1 Stamina**.
    
- You may spend **1 action** to recover **2 Stamina**.
    
- A minor rest fully restores Stamina.

### Armor

Armor does not have hit points. It acts as a gate between Stamina and Flesh/Vitality.

Armor only matters when an attack would go past Stamina and into the body. If you still have Stamina left, that Stamina absorbs the damage first.

| Armor Type   | Effect                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Light Armor  | No gate. Attacks of Minor or higher can affect Flesh/Vitality once Stamina is gone.                                                                          |
| Medium Armor | Gate: only Major or Severe attacks can affect Flesh/Vitality once Stamina is gone. Minor attacks are ignored.                                                |
| Heavy Armor  | Gate: only Severe attacks can affect Flesh/Vitality once Stamina is gone. Minor and Major attacks are ignored.<br><br>Reduces Stamina gain from action by 1. |

Shields increase PD by +1.

**Armor-piercing weapons** can reduce the gate:

- Pierce: Medium Armor is treated as Light Armor.
    
- Crush: Heavy Armor is treated as Medium Armor.

### Flesh

Flesh is a small buffer between Armor and Vitality.

Most ordinary people have **0 Flesh**.

Tough, large, or unarmored bruisers may have 1–2 Flesh.

- Losing Flesh does not cause a wound condition.
    
- Flesh represents shallow cuts and bruises.

### Vitality

Vitality represents real bodily harm: deep cuts, broken bones, punctured organs, internal bleeding.

- Normal humans have **3 Vitality**.
    
- Player characters have **4 Vitality**.
    
- Large or exceptionally tough creatures may have **5–6 Vitality**.

For every point of Vitality you are missing:

- **-1 to all rolls**
    
- **-1 meter to your speed**

## 3. Dying

When a creature reaches 0 Vitality Points it is incapacitated, can only crawl, and will die within after 10 rounds unless it receives aid. 

**Stabilize:**  
Spend 2 actions and make a successful DC 10 Medicine check. The creature will no longer die after a minute.

A creature can stabilize itself by spending 5 or more stamina at the start of its turn.

**Treat Wound:**  
Spend 2 actions outside combat, or with proper tools. Restore one point of Vitality.


## 5. Unaware and Helpless Targets

If a target is unaware of the attacker, asleep, bound, or otherwise unable to defend itself:

- **Stamina is bypassed.**
    
- The attack goes directly to Armor Gate, then Flesh/Vitality.
    
- Armor still applies.
    
- The attacker may choose the wound effect instead of rolling if they deal Vitality damage.



## 6. Psyche and Spirit Health

Psyche and Spirit damage use the same resolution structure.

Shaken happens at 4 or less Spirit or Psyche HP and reduces defense against psychic and spiritual attacks. Broken happens at 0 Spirit or Psyche HP and severely reduces spiritual and psychic defenses.

### Psyche Damage

Baseline human MD is **7**. Temporary HP, or training can raise it.


- Shaken 
    
- Broken


### Spirit Damage

Baseline human SD is **7**. Temporary HP, or training can raise it.

- Shaken
    
- Broken


## 7. Temporary Health Points

Some abilities grant temporary health points.

Temporary health points are assigned to a specific pool when gained.

Examples:

| Temp Pool | Effect While Active                                                            |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Luck      | Can be placed in front of any pool. Can be expended to reroll certain actions. |
| Bravery   | Sits in front of Psyche. Grants +2 to fear and terror checks.                  |
| Faith     | Sits in front of Spirit. Grants +2 to spiritual attacks and resistance.        |
| Willpower | Sits in front of Psyche or Spirit. Acts as temporary Mental Armor.             |

Temporary points are lost before the real pool. When the temporary points are gone, their bonus ends.

## 8. Rest and Recovery

| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Minor Rest  (1hr)    | Restore all Stamina, Flesh, 2 Spirit, and 2 Psyche.             |
| Major Rest (8hrs)    | Restore all Stamina, Flesh, 5 Spirit, 5 Psyche, and 2 Vitality. |

## 9. Combat Example

**Hero**  
PD 8, Stamina 4, Light Armor, Flesh 0, Vitality 3.

**Bandit**  
PD 6, Stamina 1, No Armor, Flesh 0, Vitality 2.

**Round 1**

Hero attacks with a shortsword.  
Rolls 1d8+2 = 6+2 = 8.  
Compare to Bandit PD 6:

- Miss: ≤6
    
- Minor: 7–8
    
- Major: 9–11
    
- Severe: 12+

Result 8 is **Minor**.  
Bandit loses **1 Stamina**.  
Bandit’s Stamina is now 0.

**Round 2**

Hero attacks again.  
Rolls 1d8+2 = 7+2 = 9.  
Compare to Bandit PD 6: 9 is **Major**.  
Damage = 2 points.

Bandit has no Stamina and no Armor, so both points go to Vitality.  
Bandit had 2 Vitality.  
Bandit’s Vitality is now 0.  
Bandit is **Dying**.

The whole fight takes two actions.

1. **Make harm legible.**  
    A hit is not an abstract subtraction from a large HP pool. It is a wound, a broken bone, or a deep cut with immediate, understandable consequences.
    
2. **Remove attack rolls and Armor Class.**  
    Combat resolution is based on rolling damage and comparing it to a defense threshold. This means every offensive action has a concrete result, and “missing” still costs the defender Stamina.
    
3. **Separate avoidance from injury.**  
    Stamina represents dodging, parrying, and deflecting. Vitality represents actual bodily harm. This prevents the “ten knife wounds to the chest” where most mortals in DND can sustain ridiculous amounts of serious bodily harm, separating combat from the narrative.
    
4. **Keep combat lethal but fair.**  
    Stamina allows characters to avoid death without making them damage sponges. Once Stamina is gone, real harm occurs quickly. Ambushes and helpless targets bypass Stamina entirely.
    
5. **Make wounds matter immediately.**  
    Missing Vitality imposes penalties to rolls and speed. Reaching 0 Vitality means incapacitation and a death timer, not just falling unconscious with abstract death saves.
    
6. **Use armor as a gate, not a miss chance.**  
    Armor determines whether an attack can reach the body once Stamina is depleted. Heavier armor blocks lesser hits but has tradeoffs.
    
7. **Support tactical choice and narrative tension.**  
    Players must manage Stamina, positioning, armor, and wound penalties. Getting hit feels dangerous, and avoiding harm is a meaningful part of combat.
