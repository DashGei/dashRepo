## 2: Stances

Gives you a passive, a reaction, and a once per turn action. A creature can switch between them with their turn start action. 

Pros: Neatly groups your maneuvers and gives you a simplified list to choose from. Constrictive enough to the point where it requires forward thinking.

Cons: Some stances may thematically clash with character archetypes who will choose them. Need to be balanced against each other.

To solve some of the cons, it could be fun to allow players to create one stance themselves at level 5 so they have at least one stance they feel is emblematic of their character's fighting style. Characters can unlock one more at levels 10, 15, and 20.

# Always available examples:

These maneuvers should always be accessible to anyone proficient with their weapon.

**Parry** {R*} When attacked with a strike dealing physical damage, you can make a melee strike with their weapon as the target. If your strike deals more damage then theirs, their strike becomes a miss.


**Trip** When you hit a creature with a melee weapon attack, you can activate this feat. The target must make a **Physical DC** Strength saving throw. On a failure, it is knocked prone.

# Definitely only a few times a day, powers.

**Take it and Leave it** When you deal Flesh or Vitality damage to a creature with a Slashing or Piercing melee weapon, you can leave the weapon in the enemy. While the weapon is lodged, the target subtracts 2d8 from all rolls they make. A creature can remove the weapon with an action, taking 2d8 Pain damage when they remove it.
