# Stealth

## Sneak

**{AA}**

A creature attempts to conceal its location by making a DT 6 Agility Check.

On a successful check, it becomes **Hidden** and establishes a **Hidden Sphere** centered on its actual location. The sphere's radius depends on the degree of success.

| Sneak Result | Hidden Sphere Radius |
| ------------ | -------------------- |
| Miss         | No sphere            |
| Minor        | 3m                   |
| Moderate     | 6m                   |
| Major        | 9m                   |
On a Miss, the creature fails to establish a Hidden Sphere. Its position remains known to observers who could perceive it.

A Hidden Sphere represents the possible location of the hidden creature. Its actual location remains real, but is not established for observers who have not located it.

## Hidden Position

At the start of each of its turns, a hidden creature may designate a new position for itself anywhere within its current Hidden Sphere. It then establishes a new Hidden Sphere of the same radius, centered on that position.

The creature chooses its own position within the sphere. It does not have to reveal that choice to observers who have not located it.

A creature may choose to remain at its current position.

**A Hidden Sphere does not grant movement or teleportation.** The creature must still obey the physical constraints of the environment. It cannot pass through walls, closed barriers, impassable terrain, or other obstacles it could not ordinarily cross.

The creature may, however, cross exposed areas without automatically revealing its actual position. This represents uncertainty about where it moved, not an absence of physical movement.

The GM determines whether a proposed position is physically reachable and plausible given the creature's previous actions and the environment.

## Search

**{AA}**

A creature attempting to locate a hidden creature makes a DT 6 Instinct Check.

Compare the Search result's degree of success to the hidden creature's Sneak result. If the search degree is equal to or higher than Sneak degree then the searcher locates the creature.

When a creature is located, the searching creature chooses a plausible position for the hidden creature within its current Hidden Sphere. That position becomes the creature's established location.

The chosen position must be consistent with the environment and the creature's possible movement. The searcher cannot place the creature through an impassable obstacle or in a position it could not physically occupy.

Locating a creature reveals its position to the searching creature. It does not automatically reveal that position to other observers.

## Revealing Actions

A hidden creature may perform actions that reveal its position, whether intentionally or unintentionally.

Examples include:

- Making a Strike that exposes its location.
    
- Shouting or making a conspicuous noise.
    
- Interacting with an object in a way that clearly reveals where it is.
    
- Performing another action that an observer could use to establish its position.
    

When a hidden creature performs a revealing action, its position is established at a plausible location within its Hidden Sphere from which it could perform that action.

The GM determines which observers can perceive the revealing action. Those observers learn the creature's position.

An action does not reveal a creature merely because it has an effect. It must provide enough perceptible information to establish the creature's location. For example, a hidden creature might pull a lever using a concealed mechanism without exposing itself, if the circumstances plausibly permit it.

## Knowledge and Hidden Position

A creature that knows a hidden creature's exact position retains that knowledge until circumstances make it unreliable.

Once an observer has located a hidden creature, that creature loses the benefit of uncertain positioning against that observer. The observer knows where it is, and the creature cannot use its Hidden Sphere to deny that observer knowledge of its location.

Other observers do not automatically gain this knowledge. An observer who has not located the hidden creature may remain unaware of its position, even when an ally knows exactly where it is.

However, the hidden creature no longer benefits from its uncertain position against observers who know where it is. Its position is not treated as unknown merely because some creatures remain unaware of it.

An observer may communicate the creature's location to allies. Those allies gain the information if they can receive and understand the communication, but they still need the appropriate line of sight or other means of perceiving the creature to interact with it normally.

## Ending Hidden Status

A creature ceases to be Hidden when it chooses to stop hiding or when circumstances make its concealment impossible.

Being located by one observer does not necessarily end Hidden status against all other observers. Instead, the creature loses the benefit of uncertain positioning against the observers who have located it.

If the creature becomes plainly visible or otherwise perceptible to everyone relevant, it is no longer Hidden.
